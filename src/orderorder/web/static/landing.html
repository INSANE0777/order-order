TAXONOMY OF FAILURE

The twelve ways a citation lies.

A citation does not fail by being simply “wrong”. It fails in twelve structured ways, each undetectable to traditional lexical search and fatal in courtroom arguments.

MODE 01 Hallucination

Phantom Precedent

The citation looks authentic with volume, reporter, and year, but the judgment does not exist in any official law report.

Detector: Registry Exact Match
MODE 02 Voice Inversion

Submission as Holding

Advocate’s argument recorded in the summary of submissions quoted as the binding ratio of the Bench.

Detector: Rhetorical Role Parser
MODE 03 Fabrication

Fabricated Pinpoint

The case exists, but the cited proposition does not appear anywhere near the indicated paragraph or page.

Detector: Paragraph Verbatim Locator
MODE 04 Precedent

Overruled Authority

The precedent was reversed by a larger bench or nullified by retrospective legislation.

Detector: Citation Graph Citator
MODE 05 Weight

Ratio vs. Obiter

A passing judicial observation claimed as a binding principle of law under Article 141 of the Constitution.

Detector: Judicial Weight Classifier
MODE 06 Distortion

Distorted Quotation

Key words, qualifications, or exceptions omitted from the quotation to change the ruling’s scope.

Detector: Word-for-Word Diff Engine
MODE 07 Hierarchy

Bench Strength Defiance

Citing a 2-judge bench decision that conflicts with an earlier, binding 5-judge Constitution Bench ruling.

Detector: Bench Seniority Hierarchy
MODE 08 Attribution

Dissenting Voice Quoted

Dissenting or concurring opinion presented as the holding of the court without disclosing the minority status.

Detector: Majority/Dissent Splitter
MODE 09 Facts

Fact Distinction Inapplicability

Relying on a precedent whose core statutory context or material facts preclude application to the dispute.

Detector: Factual Scope Matcher
MODE 10 Statutory Drift

Superseded Statute

Interpreting a statutory provision that was subsequently repealed, amended, or substituted by Parliament.

Detector: Legislative Timeline Anchor
MODE 11 Selective Reading

Contrary Line Concealment

Ignoring an immediately following proviso or exception in the subsequent paragraph of the same judgment.

Detector: Bidirectional Clause Scanner
MODE 12 Hearsay

Secondary Source Distortion

Quoting a headnote, digest, or commentary that misstated the ruling of the actual judgment.

Detector: Primary Text Corroborator
VERDICT HIERARCHY

The six-step staircase.

Every citation tested lands on a deterministic grade from A to F. Grades are never averaged or obscured. Unchecked checks stand off the stair entirely.

A

Grade A — Direct Binding Holding

The court's verbatim ratio decidendi, rendered by the majority, verified in official SCR text, and unassailed by subsequent benches.

B

Grade B — Verifiable Support with Narrowed Scope

The court supports the principle, though qualified by statutory conditions or factual reservations that must be pleaded accurately.

C

Grade C — Obiter Dictum / Persuasive Remark

A non-binding observation not essential to the determination. Safe to argue only when expressly framed as persuasive authority.

D

Grade D — Misattributed Advocate Submission

The sentence cited belongs to counsel’s arguments or summary of pleadings, rather than the judgment of the court.

E

Grade E — Overruled or Distinguishable Authority

The authority has been declared per incuriam, explicitly overruled by a larger bench, or superseded by amendment.

F

Grade F — Phantom Citation or Severe Fabrication

The citation cannot be resolved in official law reports, or the text carries no relation to the asserted legal proposition.
